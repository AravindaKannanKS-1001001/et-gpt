# Bloom/Bar/Billet Length Measurement System

## Project Overview

The Bloom/Bar/Billet Length Measurement System is a steel manufacturing solution developed for accurate, non-contact measurement of hot blooms, billets, bars, and slabs.

The system is part of the ET Xpro Vision System and is designed to improve measurement accuracy, reduce material giveaway, and support precise cutting operations in steel production plants.

The solution addresses challenges associated with manual length estimation, varying product sizes, and product movement during production.

## Manufacturing Challenge

### Existing Process

Blooms are supplied according to customer-specified lengths.

Traditionally:

- A scale is marked on the roller table.
- Operators observe bloom length from the pool pit.
- Products are manually cut to target dimensions.

### Issues Identified

#### Parallax Error

Visual estimation can introduce measurement errors because the operator observes the bloom from a distance.

#### Manual Cutting Variability

Manual cutting can result in inaccuracies.

#### Increased Material Giveaway

Measurement and cutting inaccuracies may result in excess material being supplied beyond required specifications.

---

## Measurement Technology

### Laser Doppler Gauge

The system uses a Laser Doppler Gauge to measure the length of hot blooms, billets, and slabs.

#### Operating Principle

The gauge:

1. Illuminates the product surface using an interference pattern generated by two intersecting laser beams.
2. Creates alternating bright and dark interference fringes.
3. Measures modulation of scattered light caused by object movement.
4. Detects the scattered light using a photodiode.
5. Digitally processes the resulting electrical signal.
6. Calculates object speed from the measured frequency.
7. Calculates object length by integrating speed measurements over time.

### Measurement Characteristics

- High-accuracy measurement
- Non-contact operation
- Suitable for hot products

---

## Limitation of Fixed Doppler Gauge Installation

### Standoff Distance Constraint

The Laser Doppler Gauge can measure accurately only within a specified standoff distance.

### Impact of Product Size Variation

Bloom dimensions can vary.

When product size changes:

- Distance between the product and gauge changes.
- Measurement performance may be affected if the gauge moves outside its required standoff distance.

---

## Solution Architecture

### Moving Actuator-Based Gauge Positioning

To overcome size variation challenges:

- The Doppler Gauge is mounted on a moving actuator.
- The actuator maintains the required standoff distance.
- Gauge position is adjusted according to bloom size and position.

This allows measurement across varying product dimensions.

## Handling Product Size Variations

### Position Tracking

As the bloom exits the roughing mill:

- A laser displacement sensor tracks bloom position.
- The actuator positions the Doppler Gauge at the required measurement location.

### Fine Position Adjustment

Bloom movement may not always be linear.

To compensate:

- A second laser displacement sensor is installed beside the Doppler Gauge.
- The sensor provides fine positioning corrections.
- The system compensates for zig-zag movement on the roller table.

---

## Handling Head and Tail Deviations

### Fishtail Formation

Blooms exiting the roughing mill may have irregular head and tail sections.

Excessive cutting of these sections can increase material wastage.

### HMD-Based Detection

The system uses two Hot Metal Detectors (HMDs) to:

- Detect bloom entry
- Detect bloom exit
- Reset measurement references
- Improve cutting optimization

---

## System Workflow

### Step 1: Bloom Entry Detection

- HMD-1 detects bloom entry.
- Previous measurement values are reset.

### Step 2: Position Tracking

- The laser displacement sensor tracks bloom position.
- The actuator moves the Doppler Gauge to the required measurement position.

### Step 3: Length Measurement

- The bloom reaches the Doppler Gauge.
- Measurement begins.

### Step 4: Fine Alignment

- The secondary laser displacement sensor compensates for positional deviations.
- Fine adjustments are applied when bloom movement varies.

### Step 5: Bloom Exit Detection

- HMD-2 detects the end of the bloom.
- End-of-product information is generated.
